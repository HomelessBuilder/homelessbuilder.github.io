Career: interview preparation

Recruiter follow-up questions

Prepared Oct 21, 2026. The screening questions cover any candidate. These are the follow-ups a recruiter asks when a background is mostly self-employment, a founded company, or independent projects built with AI tools, for Cloud Infrastructure Engineer and AI Engineer roles.

Verifying the experience

Recruiters can't call a former manager when the candidate was the company, so they verify by asking.

  1. Was this role paid employment, a contract, or your own company?Founder and self-titled roles are common, and recruiters have to classify them correctly before comparing a candidate to the posting's years-of-experience line.
  2. Who else worked on it, and who used it?Separates a solo lab from a production system other people depended on.
  3. Do you have paying customers or revenue?An outside party paying is the strongest proof the work met a real bar.
  4. Is it running now? Can you walk me through it?Live systems and a confident walkthrough beat any description on paper.

Credentials and eligibility

These are often hard filters, decided before skills are discussed.

  1. Your résumé lists certifications as in progress. Which have you passed, and when is your next exam?"In progress" without a date reads as unverified, and some postings require a specific certification outright.
  2. Are you a US citizen, and are you eligible for a security clearance?Government and defense roles can't waive this, so it's asked early.
  3. What compensation are you targeting, and would you consider contract work?Checks fit against the budget and whether a contract-to-hire path is open.
  4. Are you open to on-site work, relocation, or travel?Many infrastructure roles, especially cleared ones, are on-site or carry travel.

Depth behind the tool list

Skills sections list tools. These questions test how deep the experience goes.

  1. Did you define your infrastructure as code, or build it by hand?Infrastructure as code appears in nearly every cloud posting; console-built environments signal a different level.
  2. How is access controlled in your cloud account?A quick test of security habits: root account protection, MFA, roles instead of shared keys.
  3. Have you run containers or Kubernetes, even in a lab?Most cloud and AI platform roles assume container fluency.
  4. Walk me through an outage you diagnosed and fixed.Shows troubleshooting method and ownership, not just building.

Working with AI tools

Increasingly standard for any candidate, and central for AI engineering roles.

  1. How much of this code did you write, and how much did an AI tool write?Recruiters want an honest split, not a number that sounds good.
  2. Could you debug and change it without the AI tool?Tests whether the candidate understands the system or only directed its creation.
  3. How do you know the AI's output is right?Review discipline, tests and evaluations are what separate production AI work from demos.
  4. Has anyone besides you installed or used what you built?Outside users surface problems a builder never sees, and recruiters know it.
  5. What's the scale, and can you put a number on any result?Projects, users, requests, time saved, time to ship: numbers make claims checkable.

Answering them well

  • Answer with specifics: names of services, counts, dates.
  • Give the honest split between your work and an AI tool's; then show you understand the result.
  • Offer something they can look at: a repo, a live link, a short demo.
  • Have a date for every certification marked in progress, or remove it.
